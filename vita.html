Vivanco Intelligent Training Assistant.

Calibrate a lift once, then VITA reads your reps in reserve on every set, tracks fatigue across the session, and prescribes your next sets — answering the questions a lifter actually asks, in plain language.

from ablevlabs import vita

# Calibrate the bench from two all-out sets.
bench = vita.calibrate(weight1=315, reps1=5,
                       weight2=275, reps2=9, name="Bench Press")

workout = vita.Workout(bench)

# How many reps at 275 leave 2 in reserve?
workout.target_reps(weight=275, rir=2)   # 7

# Log it -- get a coach's readout back.
workout.log_set(weight=275, reps=7)
// how it works

Three steps, plain language.

Every method answers one question a lifter naturally asks. No equations to wire together — you talk to it the way you'd talk to a coach.

01

Calibrate once

Two all-out sets define a lift's strength curve. vita.calibrate(...) learns it — no max attempt, no lab.

02

Log as you lift

workout.log_set(...) records each set and hands back your reps in reserve, effort, and a plain-English verdict.

03

Let it coach

Ask target_reps, predict_set, or build_plan — it prescribes, predicts, and tracks fatigue for you.

// the readout

It answers in words, not floats.

Ask what a set will cost and you don't get 2.384728 — you get a readout you can act on, with a verdict on where the set sits.

workout.predict_set(weight=295, reps=5)

The same readout comes back from log_set after a set, and from a full workout.summary() at the end — totals, average and lowest RIR, capacity remaining, and your hardest set.

// any lift, any equipment

Barbell, dumbbell, cable — or just your bodyweight.

VITA reads only weight and reps, so the equipment is irrelevant. Loaded lifts and pure calisthenics use the same calls.

Weighted lift

Calibrate, then log

Name the lift, feed two hard sets, and you have a calibrated curve and an estimated 1RM. Then log your working sets and read the reserve off each one.

benchsquatdeadliftrowpress
squat = vita.calibrate(weight1=405, reps1=4,
                       weight2=365, reps2=8, name="Squat")
squat.one_rep_max                  # estimated, no max attempt

workout = vita.Workout(squat)
set1 = workout.log_set(weight=315, reps=6, rest=180)
set1.rir                           # reps in reserve
set1.status                        # "Good working set"
# Bodyweight: one measured fresh max, no weight
pullups = vita.calibrate_bodyweight(15, name="Pull-ups")

workout = vita.Workout(pullups)
workout.log_set(reps=10, rest=120).rir    # 5.0
workout.log_set(reps=10, rest=0).rir      # 3.75 -- tiring

No weight needed

// build today's session

It writes the workout for you.

Pick a weight and a target effort; VITA prescribes each set and drops the reps automatically as fatigue accumulates — so every set lands at the same proximity to failure instead of drifting.

build_plan

Same effort, every set

Hold 2 reps in reserve across five sets, and the rep target steps down as you tire — keeping each set productive, never junk volume and never accidental failure.

The plan is hypothetical: it never touches your live session, and exports to a DataFrame with .to_frame().

Every method is a question.

A small surface you can hold in your head — each call maps to something you'd actually ask between sets.

vita.calibrate(...)Learn a lift's strength curve from two all-out sets.
vita.calibrate_bodyweight(...)Bodyweight mode, from one measured fresh max.
workout.log_set(weight, reps, rest)"Log my set." Returns a readout with a verdict.
workout.target_reps(weight, rir)"How many reps should I do?" Returns a whole number.
workout.predict_set(weight, reps)"What will this set cost?" Returns a full prediction.
workout.build_plan(...)"Build today's session." Reps fall as fatigue rises.
workout.summary()"How did it go?" Totals, average and lowest RIR, more.
workout.capacity"How tired am I?" Your remaining capacity, 0 to 1.
// under the hood

Rigour you don't have to see.

Powered by the Vivanco Proximity-to-Failure Model

VITA's friendly surface sits on a formal, proven mathematical model — the same VPFM derivation, with its load-rep law, fatigue dynamics, and Monte Carlo intervals, all still here for power users (LoadRepCurve, total_load, rir_interval). Most lifters never need them. If you want the mathematics, read the model paper.

// research status

Where it stands.

Research preview

The engine is complete and tested — a proven core with a passing test suite, backed by the formal model and its derivations.

Still open: the fatigue constants ship as sensible defaults, not yet fit to real training data, so the numbers are well-formed estimates rather than calibrated truth — a precise starting point, treated as such.

Fitting and validating those constants against measured reps-to-failure is the roadmap: the path from a sound coach to a trusted one.
