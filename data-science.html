Predicting Powerlifting Performance

Regression28,766 recordsEnd-to-end
◆ Live dashboard + notebook
The question

Given a lifter's bodyweight, age, sex, and equipment, how much will they total — and what actually drives strength? A regression problem with a clear, testable answer.

The data

Competition records in the OpenPowerlifting schema. Real-world messy: dirty categories (mixed-case labels), impossible outliers (data-entry errors in bodyweight), and missing values (skipped ages, bombed lifts). Cleaning was ~half the work — standardizing categories, filtering implausible rows, and deciding when to drop vs impute.

The approach

Explore (distributions, correlations, relationships) → engineer features (a strength-to-bodyweight ratio, age bins, one-hot encoding of categories) → model with a proper train/test split: a Linear Regression baseline, then a Random Forest to capture the non-linear age effect → evaluate and interpret.

What it found

The random forest explained 90% of the variance in totals and predicted within ~28 kg on data it had never seen — beating the linear baseline by capturing that strength peaks in the late 20s–30s then declines. Feature importance quantified what coaches know by feel: sex and bodyweight are the dominant drivers, with equipment and age contributing less.
